Company Overview

What They Do

Aeroflex Enterprises Limited (formerly Sat Industries Ltd) is a listed holding company. It is not Aeroflex Industries, which has its own report; it owns that company. Through subsidiaries it consolidates: Aeroflex Industries (59.84% held directly; stainless-steel flexible hoses and new liquid-cooling skids for data centres), Aeroflex Neu (55.50%; FIBC and woven packaging, formerly Sah Polymers), Aeroflex Finance (100%; an RBI-registered, non-deposit-taking NBFC) and, until 30 April 2026, M.R. Organisation (MRO) (68%; air-compressor parts, sold to Ingersoll-Rand). It also runs a startup and fund investing book — 168 investments across 35+ sectors per the annual report, mostly small tickets of ₹3 lakh to ₹1 Cr — which the user calls ‘like a VC’. The numbers in §03 show why that label overstates it.

FY26 (consolidated, annual report): revenue from operations ₹697.83 Cr (+20.6% on ₹578 Cr; MarketSmith TTM sales ₹752 Cr, a different period), EBITDA ₹149.81 Cr, PAT ₹85.33 Cr (₹64.22 Cr to owners, ₹21.11 Cr to minorities), EPS ₹5.68. Standalone — the parent alone — is an investing vehicle: total income ₹20.11 Cr, PAT ₹11.03 Cr, 19 employees. MarketSmith’s blurb (‘plastics manufacturing, exports and technology services’) is the old Sat Industries description and is outdated; the donut shows the FY26 segments.

Key Competitive Advantage: Not a conventional moat. The edge is ownership: a control stake in Aeroflex Industries that is carried at a cost of ₹18.38 Cr (₹2.32 a share) and is worth about ₹4,117 Cr at ₹520 (9 Oct close; my arithmetic), plus a record of timing exits — MRO sold at about 3x in about 21 months (company: ~107% XIRR), and FY26 startup exits at 9.39x (Pee Safe) and 9.28x (EMO Energy). The operating moat is narrow and sits in Aeroflex Industries; the parent’s own advantage shows up as lumpy one-off gains. Detail in §02 and §03.

The Aeroflex Industries Stake — Explained Plainly

It is a subsidiary, not a financial investment. Aeroflex Enterprises holds 7,91,81,833 shares of Aeroflex Industries (unchanged FY25 to FY26) — 59.84% directly (my arithmetic: the 65.47% group holding less 5.63% held through Italica Global FZC). The standalone balance sheet carries it at cost: ₹18.38 Cr, about ₹2.32 a share; the consolidated accounts add it line by line, with the minority share shown separately. It is not an associate and not carried at fair value, so the ~₹4,117 Cr never appears on the parent’s balance sheet. The AR gives a market value of ₹2,000 Cr for all quoted investments at 31 Mar 2026.

At the 9 Oct close: 7,91,81,833 × ₹520 = ₹4,117.5 Cr, about 295% of AEROENTER’s market cap and ~₹364 for each AEROENTER share (priced at ₹123.31). It is the dominant asset: the next-largest listed stake (Neu) is ₹99.5 Cr. The stock’s own value is therefore mostly a bet on one other stock, and AEROFLEX itself trades at P/E ~103 and 15.4x book.

So Is It ‘Like a VC’? — Measured

The label overstates the startup side. The unquoted book ex-subsidiaries is ~₹26.7 Cr (about 2% of market cap); the consolidated financial-instruments note shows unquoted equity ₹26.99 Cr + quoted equity ₹0.25 Cr + funds ₹17.12 Cr ≈ ₹44 Cr (about 3%; my arithmetic). Realised profit on sales: ₹0.62 Cr in FY26. The startup book is a real habit — 168 names, mentoring, an ESG lens — but not what the share price is paying for.

Where the venture-style value actually is: the control stake in Aeroflex Industries, bought for ~₹2.32 a share and worth 224x that, and the MRO position built up in three tranches and sold at ~3x. That is closer to a promoter holding company that also writes small angel cheques than to a fund.

Accounting: unquoted equities are at fair value through OCI, so mark-ups never reach EPS while realised gains arrive as lumpy other income. Ind AS 113 level split: not disclosed (unquoted equity is likely Level 3 — my inference). Exceptional diminution: ₹42.47 lakh standalone; ₹95.67 lakh consolidated (including a ₹52.41 lakh labour-code charge). One standalone note does not tie: FVOCI equity ₹3,141.34 lakh (prior year ₹6,090.54 lakh) vs ₹2,692.81 lakh (₹2,133.36 lakh) in the table of the same note set.
